The part that actually mattered

This started as a theming project. It turned out the machine was being throttled by its own CPU governor — a performance governor on a 15 W laptop chip, sprinting to maximum turbo for trivial work, hitting its thermal ceiling in milliseconds, and getting cut back. Thousands of times a second.

Dell Latitude 5400, i7-8665U, UHD 620. No hardware was changed to get this.

If your machine feels laggy, check this before anything else. Spot temperature readings lie — sampling sensors every two seconds showed a comfortable 61–68 °C while the CPU was throttling a quarter of the time. The samples were landing in the valleys between millisecond spikes. Read the cumulative counters in /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpu0/thermal_throttle/ instead.

What it does

Ten numbered scripts. Each one is idempotent, guarded, and reversible.

00 Preflight

Backs up your entire dconf, then prints a hardware and thermal baseline. Run it first so you can always get back.

01 Performance

The important one. Removes forced-performance tuning, restores power-profiles-daemon, sets a sane governor and energy policy.

02 Packages

dnf speed config, RPM Fusion, full ffmpeg, hardware video decode, backup tooling.

03 Theme

WhiteSur GTK, shell, icons and cursors. Inter and JetBrains Mono. Wallpaper and macOS system sounds.

04 Extensions

Twelve GNOME extensions installed straight from extensions.gnome.org, then configured for a weak iGPU.

05 Shortcuts

macOS-style keybindings using nothing but GNOME settings. No daemon required.

06 keyd (optional)

Super becomes Cmd in every application — without breaking Ctrl+C in your terminal.

07 Voice

Replaces robotic espeak-ng with Piper neural TTS, entirely inside your home directory.

08 Snapshots

btrfs safety net via snapper, wired into dnf so every transaction is undoable.

09 Verify

Read-only end-to-end check. Tells you what actually took and what silently didn't.

Things that cost real debugging time

All of them are written up in the troubleshooting guide, with the diagnosis that actually worked.

“Enabled” is not “running”

A GNOME extension can sit in the enabled list reporting OUT OF DATE and silently never load. Check State, not the list.

keyd layers inherit

Omitting a key from a compound layer doesn’t pass it through — it falls back to the parent layer and fires the wrong thing.

Never grep for “error”

keyd check reports success as “No errors found.” A naive grep marks a valid config broken.

The wrong piper

The piper package in Fedora’s repos is a gaming-mouse tool. Piper TTS isn’t packaged for Fedora at all.
